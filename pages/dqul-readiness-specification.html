Abstract

This document specifies a readiness check that a health data holder can carry out on one release of a dataset before preparing a data quality and utility label under Article 78 of [EHDS]. For each element the label must cover, the check records two separate judgements: whether the holder has a process, and whether the holder can produce evidence of the outcome for that release. It also records where the outcome depends on organisations upstream of the holder.

What the check does not do: an Evidence available state says that a process and a release-specific measurement can be documented. It does not mean that the measured quality is good, fit for a particular secondary use, or legally compliant. An observed high missingness rate can coexist with a fully reproducible measurement.

Dataset versus release: Article 78 concerns a label associated with a dataset. Assessing an identified release is an additional architectural proposal for evidence traceability, not a separate EHDS legal requirement. A dataset-level label would need an explicitly governed method for selecting, updating and aggregating release evidence; this demonstrator does not perform that aggregation.

Already adopted: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2098 lays down HealthDCAT-AP-based minimum metadata for dataset descriptions (application from 26 March 2029). This is separate from the forthcoming label specifications under Article 78(6).

The check is organised by the six element groups in Article 78(3) of [EHDS]. The twelve dimensions developed by the QUANTUM project [QUANTUM], as used in HIQA's draft Compliance Assessment Framework for Data Quality [CAF-DQ-PILOT], are mapped onto those groups, and the Article 78(3) elements that have no QUANTUM dimension are added as provisional criteria. Results can be expressed with the W3C Data Quality Vocabulary [VOCAB-DQV] and attached to the release's description in a [HEALTHDCAT-AP] catalogue.

1. Conformance

As well as sections marked as non-normative, all authoring guidelines, diagrams, examples, and notes in this specification are non-normative. Everything else in this specification is normative.

The key words MAY, MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, and SHOULD NOT in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.

This specification has two conformance classes.

A Readiness Record is the completed result of one readiness check on one Data release. It conforms if it meets every requirement addressed to it that uses the key word MUST or MUST NOT.

An Assessment Tool is software that helps people complete, store or export Readiness Records. It conforms if every Readiness Record it produces conforms, and it meets every requirement addressed to it that uses the key word MUST or MUST NOT.

Each requirement names a verification method: test (an executable check), inspection (examination of a record, design or configuration), or review (assessment by a named accountable party).

2. Introduction

This section is non-normative.

2.1 Why a readiness check

HIQA piloted its draft framework with three Irish data holders of different types and maturity [CAF-DQ-PILOT]. Four findings shape this specification:

  1. Process is not evidence. Holders could generally describe what they do to support each dimension, such as mandatory fields and controlled code lists, but could not evidence the outcome for a release. A single rating per dimension hides this. The check therefore rates process and evidence separately.
  2. Definitions are interpreted differently. Several dimensions, notably compliance, precision and representativity, were read in materially different ways. The check records the interpretation the holder applied, so that differences are visible rather than buried in a score.
  3. Holders depend on upstream sources. Holders can assure only the data they receive. Provenance, completeness and coverage often depend on organisations they do not control. The check records each such dependency and names the upstream party.
  4. Maturity did not predict readiness. None of the participants, whatever its self-reported maturity, could yet evidence the dimensions consistently. A readiness check must therefore look at evidence for the specific release, not at organisational maturity.

2.2 Which elements are checked

Article 78(3) of [EHDS] groups the elements a label must cover, where applicable, into six groups: data documentation; technical quality; data quality management processes; coverage; information on access and provision; and information on data modifications. The QUANTUM dimensions used in the draft framework fall into four categories: access and provision, coverage, data documentation and technical quality.

Two Article 78(3) groups, data quality management processes and data modifications, have no QUANTUM dimension. Within the other groups, the Article names several elements that no QUANTUM dimension covers: uniqueness and timeliness of the data; the period covered; the average time a person appears in the dataset; and the time between collection and addition to the dataset. Conversely, two QUANTUM dimensions, coherence and precision, are not named in Article 78(3)(b).

This specification is anchored to the Regulation. Each criterion is marked with its source:

  • Art. 78 + QUANTUM the element is named in Article 78(3) and has a QUANTUM dimension, whose definition is used;
  • Art. 78 only the element is named in Article 78(3) but has no QUANTUM dimension, so the definition here is provisional;
  • QUANTUM only the dimension is in QUANTUM but not named in Article 78(3), and is kept because the draft framework uses it.

2.4 Design principles

  1. One release at a time. Quality belongs to a release of data, not to an organisation.
  2. Process and evidence are separate questions.
  3. No single score. A strong result on one criterion never offsets a weak one on another.
  4. Interpretations and dependencies are recorded, not hidden.
  5. Anchored to the Regulation; mapped to QUANTUM.
  6. Machine-readable using existing vocabularies. [VOCAB-DQV] and [PROV-O], linked to [HEALTHDCAT-AP].

3. Terminology

Dataset
For this proposal, a structured collection of electronic health data described as a persistent catalogue asset, with an identifier that remains associated with it across successive updates. This is an independent working definition, not a definition adopted by HIQA or the EU.
Data release
For this proposal, a uniquely identified and reproducibly describable state of a Dataset's content, such as an annual extract or registry snapshot. A release identifier is an evidence-traceability choice in this specification; it is not a new unit of legal obligation under the EHDS.
Element group
One of the six groups of elements in Article 78(3) of [EHDS], identified here as EG-A to EG-F.
Criterion
One element to be checked, identified as LR-01 to LR-21 and belonging to exactly one Element group.
Criteria set
The versioned list of Criteria, with their definitions, defined in 8. Criteria set 0.1. This document defines criteria set 0.1.
Process rating
A rating from P0 to P3 of how far the holder has a defined, owned and monitored process for a Criterion ().
Evidence rating
A rating from E0 to E3 of how far the holder can evidence the outcome of a Criterion for the specific Data release ().
Evidence item
A document, dataset, report or system output that supports an Evidence rating, identified so that someone else could find it.
Interpretation note
A short statement of how the holder understood a Criterion when rating it.
Upstream dependency
A statement that the outcome of a Criterion depends materially on an organisation or system outside the holder's control, naming that party.
Readiness state
A state derived from the two ratings of a Criterion: Evidence available, Evidence gap, Not ready or Not applicable (7. Derived results).
Readiness profile
The Readiness states of all Criteria in a Readiness Record, presented by Element group.
Assessor
A person who contributed ratings to a Readiness Record.
Attesting officer
The person in the data holder organisation who confirms that a Readiness Record is a fair account, at which point it becomes fixed.

6. Ratings

Each Criterion is rated twice: once for process and once for evidence. The scales are deliberately short and anchored to observable facts, so that two Assessors looking at the same organisation should reach the same rating.

Process rating
RatingNameMeaning
P0NoneNothing is done for this Criterion, or no one can say what is done.
P1InformalSomething is done, but it is not written down, or depends on particular individuals.
P2DocumentedA written procedure exists, is current, and has a named owner.
P3GovernedAs P2, and the procedure's results are monitored and the procedure itself is reviewed on a set schedule, with the last review recorded.
Evidence rating, for the Data release being assessed
RatingNameMeaning
E0NoneNo account of the outcome for this release can be given.
E1DescribedThe outcome can be described in words (for example "fields are mandatory, so data is complete"), but has not been measured or checked for this release.
E2MeasuredThe outcome has been measured or checked for this release, by a stated method, and the result is recorded.
E3VerifiableAs E2, and the method, inputs and result are retained so that a third party, such as the health data access body or HIQA, could repeat or check it.

RT-02A Criterion MAY be marked not applicable only with a recorded reason. A Criterion marked "Art. 78 + QUANTUM" SHOULD NOT be marked not applicable without the Attesting officer's confirmation. Note

Interpretation: Evidence available is an evidence-reportability state, not a qualitative judgement about whether measured values are acceptable. Fitness for a defined purpose and compliance are separate decisions requiring additional criteria and governance.

Draft-only status: Incomplete is an additional interface and export state for a Criterion that has not yet received both ratings. 8. Criteria set 0.1

For each Criterion this section gives its source, a working definition, and what process and evidence typically look like. The guidance on evidence is informative; the definitions of the rating levels in 6. Ratings govern. Definitions marked QUANTUM are restated from the dimension table in [CAF-DQ-PILOT] Appendix D. Pilot findings cite [CAF-DQ-PILOT].

8.1 EG-A Data documentation

Source: [EHDS] Art. 78(3)(a): metadata, supporting documentation, the data dictionary, formats and standards used, the data source and, where applicable, the data model.

LR-01Metadata scopeArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
Whether comprehensive, standardised metadata and a variable-level data dictionary exist for the release, at a level of detail that lets a user understand the data.
Process looks like
An owned procedure for maintaining the dataset description and dictionary, updated with each release.
Evidence looks like
E2: the dictionary covers every variable in the release, checked against the release's actual columns. E3: the description validates against [HEALTHDCAT-AP] and the check is retained.
Pilot finding
No participant used HealthDCAT-AP; dictionaries existed to varying degrees, with no plan to align with a national dictionary (§4.7.3).

LR-02Data provenanceArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
Whether the source of the data, and the processes and operations applied to it from generation to release, are documented, including the agents involved, validation routines and traceability of changes.
Process looks like
A documented data flow from each source to the release, with ownership of each step.
Evidence looks like
E2: a provenance statement for this release naming each source, extract date and transformation. E3: transformation code or logs retained and linked.
Pilot finding
Documentation is often held across departments and organisations, so holders lack oversight of it (§4.7.2). Record those parties as Upstream dependencies.

LR-03Compliance with standardsArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
Whether the formats, standards, conventions and protocols the data adheres to are documented, with a person responsible. In this specification the criterion is limited to data standards (formats, terminologies, classifications and their editions). Broader information governance and data protection compliance are out of scope (PD-04).
Process looks like
A register of the standards and code-system editions used, with a named owner.
Evidence looks like
E2: the release's documentation states each format and code-system edition, checked against the data. E3: conformance checks are retained.
Pilot finding
Read variously as GDPR compliance, information governance or coding standards (§4.7.1). Hence the scope above.

8.2 EG-B Technical quality

Source: [EHDS] Art. 78(3)(b): completeness, uniqueness, accuracy, validity, timeliness and consistency of the data.

LR-04CompletenessArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
The degree to which information that could be present in the release is present, at record and variable level.
Process looks like
Defined completeness checks and targets, with a scope declared under UA-04.
Evidence looks like
E2: missingness rates per in-scope variable for this release. E3: the query or script and its output retained.
Pilot finding
Mandatory fields support completeness, but no participant had standard targets, and they asked whether reporting covers all variables or a minimum set (§4.8.3).

LR-05UniquenessArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
The degree to which each real-world entity, such as a person or an episode of care, is represented once only in the release, and duplicates are detected and resolved.
Process looks like
A documented duplicate-detection and resolution rule, including the matching method.
Evidence looks like
E2: the number of suspected and resolved duplicates in this release. E3: the matching logic and results retained.
Pilot finding
Not tested in the pilot; participants mentioned identifying duplicates as an accuracy practice (§4.8.1).

LR-06AccuracyArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
The degree to which observations correctly describe what they were designed to measure.
Process looks like
Defined checks, such as range and plausibility rules, re-abstraction audits on a sample, or comparison with a reference source.
Evidence looks like
E2: results of a sample audit or of plausibility checks for this release, with the sample size stated. E3: audit protocol and results retained.
Pilot finding
Holders cannot validate every record, especially where the source is paper, and asked what is expected (§4.8.1). A stated sampling method meets E2.

LR-07ValidityArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
The degree to which data in the release conforms to the specification of its data model or models. Under Article 78(3)(a), the data model itself is part of documentation.
Process looks like
A data model or schema that the release is validated against, with an owner.
Evidence looks like
E2: a conformance report for this release against the data model. E3: the schema and validation output retained.
Pilot finding
No participant used a standardised data model, and some were unsure what one is (§4.8.6). Where there is no model, rate Process P0 or P1.

LR-08TimelinessArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
Whether the release is as current as its stated update schedule and reference period lead users to expect.
Process looks like
A published release schedule and reference period, with an owner.
Evidence looks like
E2: the release's actual date against its scheduled date, and the latest event date it contains. E3: release logs retained.
Note
Overlaps with LR-19, which measures the lag between collection and addition. See the issue below.

LR-09ConsistencyArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
The degree to which data is plausible and uniform with other data in the release, with other sources and over time.
Process looks like
Defined cross-field and cross-release checks, and change control over variables.
Evidence looks like
E2: the results of consistency checks for this release, including against the previous release. E3: check definitions and outputs retained.
Pilot finding
Checks are embedded in operations rather than formalised; historic data contains legacy inconsistencies (§4.8.4).

LR-10CoherenceQUANTUM only

Definition
How far different parts of the dataset are uniform in format, meaning and method over time, including the stability of the data model and changes of coding system.
Process looks like
A change log of coding systems, definitions and methods, with mappings between editions.
Evidence looks like
E2: a statement of changes affecting comparability between this release and earlier ones. E3: the mappings retained.
Pilot finding
Participants saw overlap with consistency (§4.8.2).

LR-11PrecisionQUANTUM only

Definition
The degree of approximation by which data can represent reality, such as the granularity of dates and measurements and the specificity of codes.
Process looks like
Documented precision for each variable, such as date to the day or code to four characters, enforced at entry.
Evidence looks like
E2: for key variables, the proportion of values at the documented precision, such as the share of diagnoses coded to the full code. E3: the query and results retained.
Pilot finding
The most difficult dimension to interpret (§4.8.5). The evidence example above is a proposed reading.
Issue 1: Timeliness appears twice in the Regulation

Article 78(3)(b) names timeliness as technical quality, and Article 78(3)(e) separately asks for the time between collection and addition to the dataset. This specification treats the first as currency against schedule (LR-08) and the second as processing lag (LR-19). The implementing act may settle this differently.

8.3 EG-C Data quality management processes

Source: [EHDS] Art. 78(3)(c): the level of maturity of data quality management processes, including review and audit processes and examination of biases. No QUANTUM dimension corresponds.

LR-12Data quality framework and governanceArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
Whether the holder has a data quality framework covering this Dataset, with assigned responsibility, defined indicators, and a route for reporting and acting on results.
Process looks like
An adopted framework with a named data quality lead and reporting to a governance body.
Evidence looks like
E2: the data quality report for this release, as received by the governance body. E3: the minutes or action log showing it was considered.
Pilot finding
Only one of three participants had a formal framework; two had a designated data quality role (§4.3).

LR-13Review and auditArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
Whether data quality processes for this Dataset are periodically reviewed or audited, internally or externally, and findings are acted on.
Process looks like
A review or audit schedule, with a named reviewer independent of day-to-day operation.
Evidence looks like
E2: the most recent review or audit report and its date. E3: the actions arising and their status.
Note
This criterion is closest to IMMAT Standard 3.2 [IMMAT-PILOT]. An IMMAT rating is not evidence for it.

LR-14Examination of biasArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
Whether the holder has examined the release for systematic biases arising from how data is collected, such as differences in recording between sites, groups or periods, and has documented what it found.
Process looks like
A documented approach to identifying known sources of bias for this Dataset.
Evidence looks like
E2: a statement of known biases and their likely direction for this release. E3: supporting analyses retained.

8.4 EG-D Coverage

Source: [EHDS] Art. 78(3)(d): the period, population coverage and, where applicable, representativity of the population sampled, and the average time a natural person appears in the dataset.

LR-15Period coveredArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
The time period the release covers, and any gaps or changes in coverage within it.
Process looks like
Defined reference periods for each release.
Evidence looks like
E2: first and last event dates in the release, with any known gaps. E3: the query retained.

LR-16Population coverageArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
The percentage of the eligible population that the release includes.
Process looks like
A defined eligible population and denominator source.
Evidence looks like
E2: the coverage rate for this release, with the denominator source stated. E3: the calculation retained.
Pilot finding
Well understood but not routinely reported; gaps arise mainly from private-sector activity outside holders' sight (§4.6.1). Record as an Upstream dependency.

LR-17Population representativityArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
How closely the observed population represents the expected population. Article 78(3)(d) requires this only where applicable.
Process looks like
For sample-based Datasets, a sampling design and weighting method. For Datasets driven by service use or clinical need, a statement of who is systematically excluded.
Evidence looks like
E2: a comparison of the release's distribution by age, sex and region with a reference population, or a documented reason why representativity does not apply. E3: the comparison retained.
Pilot finding
Hard to apply to datasets based on service use or clinical need (§4.6.2). Such Datasets may be marked not applicable under RT-02, with reasons.

LR-18Average time a person appearsArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
The average length of time over which a person is observed in the Dataset, from first to last record.
Process looks like
A stable person identifier across releases that allows follow-up time to be calculated.
Evidence looks like
E2: the mean and median follow-up time for persons in this release. E3: the calculation retained.
Note
Not meaningful for Datasets without a person identifier; mark not applicable with reasons.

8.5 EG-E Access and provision

Source: [EHDS] Art. 78(3)(e): the time between collection of the data and their addition to the dataset, and the time to provide data following a data permit or data request approval.

LR-19Collection-to-addition lagArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
The time between an event being recorded at source and its addition to the Dataset.
Process looks like
Recorded timestamps for receipt and loading of source data.
Evidence looks like
E2: the median and 90th-percentile lag for records in this release. E3: the calculation retained.

LR-20Accessibility and time to provideArt. 78 + QUANTUM

Definition
Whether a data access and usage policy is available when the release is made available, and the average time from an approved request to delivery of data.
Process looks like
A published access policy and a tracked request log with defined start and end points (PD-05).
Evidence looks like
E2: the average time to provide, over a stated period, using the start point set in PD-05. E3: the request log retained.
Pilot finding
Holders measure start points differently, and open-data provision is not counted (§4.5.1).

8.6 EG-F Data modifications

Source: [EHDS] Art. 78(3)(f): merging and adding data to an existing dataset, including links with other datasets. No QUANTUM dimension corresponds.

LR-21Merging, adding and linkageArt. 78 only

Definition (provisional)
Whether data merged into or linked with the release from other datasets is identified, with the linkage method and its quality.
Process looks like
A documented linkage procedure, naming the linked datasets and the matching method.
Evidence looks like
E2: for this release, the datasets linked and the match rate achieved. E3: the linkage specification and quality report retained.
Note
Mark not applicable if nothing is merged or linked.

9. Attestation and provenance

AP-01A Readiness Record MUST record each Assessor's name and role, the date the assessment was completed, the Attesting officer and the attestation date.

Source: accountability · Verify: inspection

AP-02Once attested, a Readiness Record MUST NOT be changed. A correction MUST be made by a new Readiness Record that states which record it replaces.

Source: integrity · Verify: test

AP-03A Readiness Record MUST state the version of the Criteria set it used.

Source: traceability · Verify: inspection

AP-04An Assessment Tool SHOULD allow several Assessors in the same organisation to contribute to one Readiness Record, recording who set each rating.

Source: [IMMAT-PILOT] §4.2.1, §4.3.2 · Verify: test

10. Representation

A Readiness Record is represented in RDF using [VOCAB-DQV] for ratings and [PROV-O] for who did what and when. Each Criterion is a dqv:Dimension, each Element group a dqv:Category, and each Criterion has two dqv:Metrics, one for process and one for evidence. Each rating is a dqv:QualityMeasurement computed on the Data release. The terms are defined in A. Vocabulary.

RP-01An Assessment Tool MUST be able to export a Readiness Record as RDF in Turtle or JSON-LD that conforms to A. Vocabulary.

Source: principle 6 · Verify: test

RP-02Each rating MUST be a dqv:QualityMeasurement whose dqv:computedOn is the IRI of the Data release, and whose dqv:value is an xsd:integer from 0 to 3.

Source: [VOCAB-DQV] · Verify: test

RP-03A Readiness Record MUST NOT be typed as dqv:QualityCertificate, and MUST NOT use any term that presents it as a data quality and utility label.

Source: DR-06 · Verify: inspection

RP-04An Assessment Tool MUST also be able to export a Readiness Record as CSV, with one row per Criterion.

Source: [IMMAT-PILOT] §4.2.1 (downloadable version) · Verify: test

RP-05A Readiness Record MAY be linked from the description of the Data release in a [HEALTHDCAT-AP] catalogue using dqv:hasQualityMeasurement, only if the data holder chooses to publish it (PD-01).

Source: proposed optional linkage from independently identified release evidence to the dataset description · Verify: inspection

11. Assessment Tool

AT-01An Assessment Tool MUST show the definition and guidance for each Criterion, and the definitions of the rating levels, where the rating is entered.

Source: [CAF-DQ-PILOT] §4.4; [IMMAT-PILOT] §4.2.3 · Verify: test

AT-02An Assessment Tool MUST allow work to be saved and resumed before attestation.

Source: [IMMAT-PILOT] §4.2.1 · Verify: test

AT-03An Assessment Tool MUST refuse to finalise a Readiness Record that does not meet RT-01 to RT-06, and MUST list every unmet requirement in one place.

Source: testability · Verify: test

AT-04An Assessment Tool SHOULD process Readiness Records on the user's own device, and MUST NOT transmit them elsewhere without the user's explicit action.

Source: data minimisation · Verify: inspection

AT-05An Assessment Tool's user interface MUST meet [EN301549], which incorporates [WCAG21] Level AA.

Source: public sector accessibility obligations · Verify: test (audit)

12. Revision of the criteria set

RL-01The Criteria set MUST be revised to match the implementing acts adopted under Article 78(6) of [EHDS], and any delegated act under Article 78(5), within P-01 of their adoption.

Source: [EHDS] Art. 78(5), 78(6) · Verify: review

RL-02Each revision of the Criteria set MUST publish a mapping from the previous version's Criteria to the new ones, so that Readiness Records made under different versions can be compared.

Source: continuity · Verify: review

RL-03Criterion identifiers MUST NOT be reused for a different meaning in a later version.

Source: traceability · Verify: review

D. Demonstrator

This section is non-normative.

This appendix contains a working Assessment Tool for trying the check. It reads the Criteria from 8. Criteria set 0.1 in this document, so it cannot drift from them. Everything stays in your browser. Work in progress is kept in this browser's local storage where available; export draft records at any point to keep a copy. It is a demonstration, not a conforming Assessment Tool: it does not implement authenticated reviewer approval, finalisation or attestation (AP-01/AP-02), multiple Assessors (AP-04) or an accessibility audit (AT-05). Validation is a prototype check only.

Working and validation are separate: draft CSV and Turtle exports are available even when criteria are incomplete. The Validate draft action lists outstanding requirements without issuing a certificate, quality grade, attestation or legal compliance finding. RDF exports from unhosted local copies use a provisional vocabulary URI, not a persistent namespace.
