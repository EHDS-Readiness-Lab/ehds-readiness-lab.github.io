The IMMAT and CAF-DQ pilot reports, read together

HIQA published both pilot reports on 9 November 2026 as part of HealthData@IE Work Package 8. Read separately, each describes a tool. Read together, they describe one readiness programme, and show where its parts do not yet connect.

The two pilots side by side

The pilots share a work package, a steering group, a working group, a sampling frame and an analysis method. They differ in what they test and who uses the instrument.

Regulatory context: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2098 has already established minimum HealthDCAT-AP metadata requirements for dataset descriptions. It is distinct from the forthcoming implementing acts for the Article 78 Data Quality and Utility Label.

Points to consider
  • The IMMAT pilot tested usability. The CAF-DQ pilot tested whether holders could actually do what is required. The difference in tone between the reports follows from that, not from the participants.
  • Both drew participants to span self-reported maturity. The CAF-DQ found maturity made no difference to readiness (§4.9). That bears directly on how much weight a self-reported IMMAT score can carry.
  • The IMMAT sessions were described as "think-out-loud", but participants reviewed the tool beforehand and discussed it afterwards, so the method observed opinions rather than live reasoning.

How the IMMAT score is built

Each of the 12 standards is scored from 1 to 5. The scores are added to give a total from 12 to 60, which maps to a band (IMMAT report, Table 2). The settings below start at HIQA's own worked example in Figure 3. Change any level to see how the total, the band and the profile respond.

Points to consider
  • In HIQA's own example, Standards 3.1 (data standards) and 4.3 (performance assurance and risk) are at Level 1, yet the organisation is "Defined". A total lets strong standards offset weak ones.
  • The pilot found the same masking between datasets (§4.3.1). The proposed fix, dataset-level completion, addresses that but not the masking across standards.
  • The Initial band covers a single possible score, 12. One standard above Level 1 makes an organisation "Emerging".
  • Adding ordinal levels treats each step as equal in size and each standard as equal in weight; neither is argued in the report.

What the label must cover, and what the dimensions cover

Article 78(3) of the EHDS Regulation lists the elements a data quality and utility label covers, where applicable, in six groups. The CAF-DQ uses the 12 QUANTUM dimensions in four categories (CAF-DQ report, Figure 1 and Appendix D). This map sets one against the other.

Article 78(3)(a)–(f), Regulation (EU) 2025/327. Detailed technical specifications are due by implementing acts by 26 March 2027 (Art. 78(6)).

Points to consider
  • Two of the six Article groups, data quality management processes and data modifications, have no QUANTUM dimension, so the draft CAF-DQ does not test them.
  • The pilot's headline finding is that holders can evidence processes rather than outcomes (§5). The Regulation expressly includes the maturity of data quality management processes. Part of the reported mismatch comes from the taxonomy used, not only from holders' readiness.
  • The CAF-DQ's final version (October 2027) falls after the implementing acts are due, so it can be aligned with the adopted specification rather than QUANTUM's proposal.

Process and evidence, dimension by dimension

Points to consider
  • For seven of the twelve dimensions the measurement in Appendix D asks whether something is documented. The difficulty participants report is partly that "documented" is not defined. A specification of acceptable evidence would resolve much of it.
  • The report attributes difficulty to Ireland's lower digital maturity (§4.2) and plans to feed that back to QUANTUM (§6). Several difficulties, such as the scope of compliance and representativity for service-use data, arise from the definitions themselves and would apply in any Member State.
  • Upstream dependency (§4.3, §4.7.2) affects provenance, completeness and coverage. Neither report says how a label, or a review, should treat outcomes the holder does not control.

Questions the reports leave open

These are matters on which neither report reaches a position. They are stated as questions, with the body best placed to answer them.

Will the IMMAT be completed per organisation, per dataset, or both, and if per dataset, keyed to National Health Dataset Catalogue identifiers?

HIQA · IMMAT §4.3.1, §5.1

How will an IMMAT data quality score (Standard 3.2, one item on a 5-level scale) be compared with CAF-DQ findings across 12 dimensions on a 4-level scale?

HIQA · IMMAT §1.3

Will IMMAT results inform the prioritisation of reviews, and if so, how will HIQA address the incentive that creates for self-reporting?

HIQA · IMMAT §1.3, §4.4.2

Will the final CAF-DQ cover all six Article 78(3) groups, including data quality management processes and data modifications?

HIQA, following the Article 78(6) implementing acts

What evidence will satisfy a "documented" measurement, and at what granularity: all variables or a defined minimum set?

HIQA and the EU, via QUANTUM · CAF-DQ §4.8.3, §4.8.4

How will outcomes that depend on upstream data providers be attributed in a label and in a targeted review?

Department of Health, HIQA · CAF-DQ §4.3, §4.7.2

How will metadata readiness, including HealthDCAT-AP adoption and alignment with a national data dictionary, be supported before 2029?
