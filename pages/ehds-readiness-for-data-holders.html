Guide for health data holders in Ireland

Getting ready for the EHDS: what HIQA's two pilots mean for you

On 9 November 2026 HIQA published reports on two tools it is building to help data holders prepare for secondary use of health data under the European Health Data Space. This guide explains what each tool is, what the pilots found, and what you can start on now.

What happens when

Different regulatory tracks: minimum dataset catalogue metadata is specified by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2098; the detailed rules for the Data Quality and Utility Label are still due under EHDS Article 78(6). A metadata description is not itself a quality label.

Two tools, two different jobs

The IMMAT is something you fill in about your organisation. The CAF-DQ is what HIQA's reviewers will use when they check your data quality. Both sit inside HealthData@IE, the national EHDS project led by the Department of Health.

Information Management Maturity Assessment Tool

Who fills it in
You, the data holder. The pilot found it usually takes a team, typically people with data governance or data quality roles.
What it asks
Twelve questions, one for each of HIQA's 2024 National Standards for Information Management. For each, you pick the one of five statements that best describes your practice, from Initial to Optimised.
What you get
A score for each standard and an overall score between 12 and 60, banded into five maturity levels. HIQA plans to let you compare yourself anonymously with similar organisations.
When
A digital version is being built on the same platform and login as the National Health Dataset Catalogue. Roll-out to high-priority data holders is planned for 2027.
How results will be used
To help you prioritise, and, according to the report, to help HIQA plan its compliance reviews and targeted reviews of data quality.

Source: IMMAT pilot report (Deliverable 8.2), sections 1.3, 4.3.2, 5.2 and Appendix C.

Compliance Assessment Framework for Data Quality

Who uses it
HIQA reviewers, when carrying out targeted reviews of data quality at data holders.
What it covers
Twelve data quality dimensions developed for the EU's data quality and utility label by the QUANTUM project, in four groups: access and provision, coverage, data documentation, and technical quality.
How it judges
Against HIQA's four compliance levels: Compliant, Substantially compliant, Partially compliant, Non-compliant.
When
The draft is being revised. HIQA expects to publish a final version and supporting guidance in 2027, and will set out its review method before targeted reviews begin.
Why it matters
Many data holders will have to publish a data quality and utility label for their datasets. HIQA's reviews are intended to check that labels are reliable and to drive improvement.

Source: CAF-DQ pilot report (Deliverable 8.9), sections 1.3, 1.4 and 6.

What the pilots found

Both pilots were small: four data holders for the IMMAT and three for the CAF-DQ, chosen to cover different types and levels of maturity. Their findings are indications rather than national measurements.

IMMAT pilot

Participants found the tool useful, and asked for clearer guidance.

  • It helped them see where to improve and could brief senior management.
  • Organisations with several datasets found that a single organisation-wide score hid weak datasets behind strong ones. They want to assess per dataset as well.
  • They wanted worked examples for each maturity level, because choosing between levels involved judgement.
  • Time and staff capacity were the main barriers.
  • They wanted any benchmarking to be anonymous, and HIQA to be clear about how results will be used.

IMMAT report, sections 4.1 to 4.4.

CAF-DQ pilot

No participant was yet able to report against all twelve dimensions, whatever its maturity.

  • Holders were doing many of the right things, such as mandatory fields and controlled code lists, but could show the process rather than the outcome for their data.
  • Several dimensions were read in different ways, especially compliance, precision and representativity.
  • Holders often depend on the data they receive from others, which limits what they can document or improve.
  • Only one of the three had a formal data quality framework. None used HealthDCAT-AP, the EU metadata standard for health datasets.

CAF-DQ report, sections 4.3, 4.4, 4.7.3 and 4.9.

Quick self-check for one dataset

The data quality and utility label has to cover six groups of elements, set out in Article 78(3) of the EHDS Regulation. For one dataset, answer two questions per group: do you have a written process, and could you show results for your latest release? Your answers stay in this browser.

This is a rough guide, not an assessment. It does not show compliance with anything. The six groups follow the Regulation; the detailed criteria will be set by the European Commission, due by 26 March 2027.

What you can start on now

None of these depends on the final tools. Tick them off as you go; ticks are kept in this browser only.

    Still being decided

    Neither report settles these points. Watch for HIQA guidance on them.

    Organisation or dataset?

    Whether the IMMAT is completed once for your organisation, for each dataset, or both. The pilot found both are needed.

    How IMMAT results are used

    Participants asked HIQA to be clear. The report says results will help HIQA plan reviews.

    What counts as evidence

    What you will need to show for each data quality dimension, and in how much detail.

    All variables or a minimum set

    Whether completeness and consistency are reported for every variable or a defined subset.

    Measuring time to provide data

    Whether the clock starts when a request is submitted or when it is complete.

    The final label specification

    The Commission's technical specifications, due by 26 March 2027, will fix what the label contains.
